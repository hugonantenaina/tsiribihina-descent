Why the Tsiribihina

The slow road through the wild west

No roads follow the Tsiribihina. For three days, the river is the only way through: you drift between red cliffs and green gorges, stop at villages where few travellers go, and fall asleep on a sandbank under the Milky Way.

Real wilderness

Gorges, a waterfall and natural pool to swim in, lemurs in the riverside trees, herons and kingfishers along the banks.

Sakalava villages

The river is the main road for the people who live here. You stop in villages and see daily life along the water.

Nights under the stars

Two nights camping on wild sandbanks, meals cooked fresh by the crew, and a big campfire on the second night.

The classic descent

Three days, day by day

  1. Day 1

    Miandrivazo — onto the river

    Meet your crew in Miandrivazo and board your pirogue or chaland. Lunch on the water as the river enters its first gorges. First camp on a quiet sandbank, dinner cooked fresh by the camp crew.

  2. Day 2

    The heart of the Tsiribihina

    A full day on the river: swim at the Anosin'Ampela waterfall and natural pool, look for lemurs, herons and kingfishers along the banks, and visit a Sakalava village. In the evening, a big campfire on a wild sandbank and a night under the stars.

  3. Day 3

    Down to Belo-sur-Tsiribihina

    A last calm stretch of river at sunrise, then arrival in Belo-sur-Tsiribihina. Your 4x4 is waiting to take you to Morondava, Kirindy Forest or the Tsingy.

Questions

Before you go

How long is the Tsiribihina River descent?

The classic descent from Miandrivazo to Belo-sur-Tsiribihina takes 3 days and 2 nights on the river. You can add Kirindy Forest, the Avenue of the Baobabs or the Tsingy de Bemaraha after the river.

What is the difference between a pirogue and a chaland?

A pirogue is a narrow traditional wooden canoe paddled by boatmen: silent and close to the water. A chaland is a large motorised boat with a roof: more space, shade from sun and rain, and room for luggage — ideal for families and groups.

When can you do the Tsiribihina descent?

From about April to November, during the dry season. June to October has the most reliable weather. From December to March, the rains make the river and the roads unpredictable.

Where do we sleep on the river?

In tents on sandbanks along the river. The camp crew sets up the camp, cooks the meals and provides an enclosed toilet. The second night includes a big campfire.

Is the river trip safe for children?

Yes, with the right boat. Families usually choose the chaland: it is stable and spacious, and life jackets are provided for everyone.
