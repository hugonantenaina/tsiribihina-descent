Pirogue or Chaland?

Same river, same camps, same waterfall. Two very different ways to travel.

Plan my river trip Chat on WhatsApp

The pirogue: silent and close to the river

The pirogue is the traditional wooden canoe of the Tsiribihina, paddled by Malagasy boatmen who know every bend of the river. You sit low, close to the water, in near silence — the best way to hear the birds and see wildlife on the banks. Space is limited, so it suits one or two adventurous travellers per boat.

The chaland: a relaxed river cruise

The chaland is a large, flat-bottomed boat with a motor and a solid roof. You can stand, stretch out and move around, you are protected from the sun and the rain, and your luggage has room to stay dry. The motor also means shorter days on the water and more time at the waterfall, in the villages and at camp. We work with trusted chaland operators on the river, including Dafimavo, La Sirène and Lakana Be.

Our advice: travelling as a family, with older travellers or as a group of four or more? Choose the chaland. Travelling alone or as a couple and looking for adventure? Choose the pirogue.

Questions about the boats

What is the difference between a pirogue and a chaland?

A pirogue is a narrow traditional wooden canoe paddled by boatmen: silent and close to the water. A chaland is a large motorised boat with a roof: more space, shade from sun and rain, and room for luggage — ideal for families and groups.

Is the river trip safe for children?

Yes, with the right boat. Families usually choose the chaland: it is stable and spacious, and life jackets are provided for everyone.
